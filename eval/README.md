# fit4 sweep oracle (baseline ground truth)

Baseline = the unmodified sweep from branch `gui-sketches` at commit 9905ef9 (worktree `~/code/fit4-wt/baseline`), plus a pure-logging patch (`baseline-dump.patch`: per-try timings and per-candidate scores into a JSON via `FIT4_DUMP`). Every window ran as `fit4 <src> --start S --duration D --no-audio` (4 MiB, 4chan board, default grid 2 codecs x 4 heights x 2 fps, `--jobs 4`, 2 finals), alone, as `flock /tmp/fit4-heavy.lock local-job -- ...` on the desktop host (Ryzen 7 9800X3D, quota 14 cores, 16 threads). Raw per-window data: `oracle/<id>.json` (full dump: every try with target kbps, size, seconds, pass1/pass2 seconds; scores; stderr tail). Details below are generated by `summarize_oracle.py`.

What was not checked: wall times are single runs (encodes are deterministic: re-running w1 and w2 gave byte-identical sizes and XPSNR within 0.008 dB, wall time within 4%), and other jobs on the host ran under the same lock only, so unlocked background load by other agents (visible as load 5 to 17 between holds) could have nudged the numbers a few percent. XPSNR is the only quality judgement; nobody looked at the encoded clips.

### Findings that matter for the faster strategy

- **VP9 at full frame rate wins every window except w3.** The best screening candidate was vp9-<h>p60 in 7 of 8 (w3's winner is vp9 at 30 fps: a near-static screencast). The 30 fps candidates trail the best 60 fps one by 2.5 to 6.1 dB in the other seven windows (XPSNR is measured at the source rate), and x264 never won (the best x264 final trails the VP9 winner by 0.04 to 1.8 dB; within 0.7 dB in w2, w3 and w6).
- **Winner height varies**: 1080 (w1, w5, w6), 900 (w4), 720 (w2, w8; w3 is a native 720p source), 2048 (w7, a 2162-tall source). Tallest is not always best: in the three hardest windows (w2, w4, w8) 1080p60 was infeasible and 720p/900p won.
- **The final stage adds almost nothing for VP9.** Finals reuse the screening settings (`cpu-used 3`), so the VP9 final repeats the screening encode: 1165 job-s in finals, of which most is VP9 re-encoding the same thing (identical size and score in all 8 windows). The x264 final (veryslow) lifts the x264 screening score by 0.0 to 0.9 dB but never reached VP9.
- **Infeasible VP9 candidates are the biggest waste on hard footage**: w8 spent 67% of its work on 3 infeasible candidates, w4 32% on one, w2 29% on one; easy windows have none. The infeasible ones are always full-height or near-full-height VP9 (1080p60 in w2, w4, w8; also 1080p30 and 900p60 in w8) with budgets of 1.26 to 1.37 Mbps.
- **Size correction is common**: 52 of 123 feasible screening candidates needed at least one retry; 15 used all three tries. Retries cost 15% of all work overall (65% on w3, where VP9 and x264 both mispredict by 30 to 100% on the first try because the screencast is highly compressible).
- **VP9 is 80% of screening work; pass 1 is only 12% of VP9 time**, so reusing pass-1 stats saves roughly that fraction of each retry (a smaller win than the 1.3 to 1.6x guessed in the research note), while the infeasibility and retry shares above are the larger levers.
- **Scoring (XPSNR + SSIM at source size) costs 2106 job-s**, about a quarter of the total work; it runs per candidate and per final.
- **sshfs source effect is small**: w1 402 s (source on the pool) vs 386 s (local copy); w2 635 vs 630. The kept oracle numbers all use a local copy of the source, staged before the lock.
